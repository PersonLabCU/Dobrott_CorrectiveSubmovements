 Paper / Publication

Dissociable cerebellar pathways for reach correction and learning

in press

Purpose / Summary

Sensory feedback can trigger rapid motor corrections, but how self-generated movement errors are corrected and influence subsequent behavior remains less clear. Here, we found that corrective submovements (CSMs) – structured, feedback-driven adjustments – occurred either spontaneously or in response to neural perturbations during mouse reaching. Corrections were modestly encoded in the cerebellar anterior interposed nucleus (IntA) along with non-corrective reach kinematic correlates. Closed-loop perturbations of red nucleus-projecting IntA neurons triggered errors that were countered by CSMs and led to learned shifts in the timing of future submovements, even in the absence of further perturbation. In contrast, closed-loop perturbations of non-specific IntA neurons induced errors followed by inaccurate corrections and no learning. Thus subsets of IntA outputs differentially permit or corrupt accurate corrections respectively.
